# Voice

## Tone in one line
The training partner who read the study, tried the thing, and tells you straight — curious, slightly obsessive, never preachy.

## Sample lines (the ratio and register to match)

- "Berlin mein is weekend jo hua, uska ek number mujhe soney nahi de raha — 2:01:xx. [F2] Aur yeh sirf shoes ki baat nahi hai."
- "Seedhi baat: agar aap hafte mein teen din daudte ho aur do din lift karte ho, toh yeh study aapke liye hi hai. [F5]"
- "Sach bolu toh, maine yeh protocol khud try kiya — [YUGANSH: what happened on your Tuesday tempo]."
- "Zone 2 is boring. Bhai, it's supposed to be boring. That's the point."
- "Matlab yeh insaan hai ya machine? 101 Ironmans, 101 days. [F1]"

## Do
- Lead with the specific number, name, or moment. Then explain why a runner-who-lifts should care.
- Short spoken sentences. Read every line aloud in your head; if you'd run out of breath, split it.
- Use Hindi for emphasis, emotion, and the "friend talking" feel. Keep every technical term in English.
- Own opinions as opinions: "mujhe lagta hai", "my take", "I could be wrong here".
- One CTA at the end, spoken like a person, ideally a question that invites comments.

## Don't
- "In today's video...", "Welcome back to the channel", "Smash that like button", "Don't forget to subscribe" as openers.
- "You won't believe", "This changes everything", "The secret that pros don't want you to know", "game-changer", "insane" (as a hype filler — fine when literally describing 101 Ironmans).
- Devanagari script. Forced Hindi where English is more natural. Translating technical terms.
- Medical or nutrition prescriptions ("you should take X grams"). Report what the study did; the viewer decides.
- Claims about Yugansh's own training that he hasn't supplied. Always `[YUGANSH: ...]`.
